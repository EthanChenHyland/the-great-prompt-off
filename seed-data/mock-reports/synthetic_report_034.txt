MRI RIGHT KNEE

Clinical indication: Chronic knee pain. Evaluation for internal derangement.

Technique: Multiplanar, multisequence MR imaging of the right knee was performed without intravenous contrast.

Findings:

Medial compartment:
There is severe medial femorotibial osteoarthrosis with diffuse high-grade to full-thickness cartilage loss, bulky marginal osteophytes, and subchondral cystic/reactive marrow change. The medial meniscus is macerated and extruded.

Lateral compartment:
Multifocal high-grade chondral loss and marginal osteophyte formation are present in the lateral femorotibial compartment. The lateral meniscus is diffusely degenerated, with complex tearing of the body and anterior horn.

Femoropatellar joint:
Severe femoropatellar osteoarthrosis is present, greatest at the lateral patellar facet and trochlea, with high-grade cartilage loss and subchondral marrow edema. The extensor mechanism is intact.

Cruciate ligaments:
The anterior cruciate ligament is chronically attenuated without an acute pivot-shift osseous injury pattern. The posterior cruciate ligament is intact.

Collateral ligaments and posterolateral corner:
The collateral ligaments are intact. The medial collateral ligament is chronically bowed by medial osteophytes.

Soft tissues:
There is a moderate joint effusion with synovial proliferation. A small Baker cyst is present. No acute fracture is identified.

Impression:
1. Severe tricompartmental osteoarthrosis, greatest in the medial and femoropatellar compartments.
2. Macerated, extruded medial meniscus and complex degenerative tearing of the lateral meniscus.
3. Moderate effusion with synovitis and small Baker cyst.
4. Chronically attenuated ACL without acute osseous injury pattern.
