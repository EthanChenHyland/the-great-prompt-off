RIGHT KNEE MRI

CLINICAL INFORMATION: Chronic knee pain and swelling.

TECHNIQUE: Routine multiplanar MRI of the right knee was performed without contrast.

FINDINGS:
There is no acute fracture or osteonecrosis. Alignment is maintained.

Tricompartmental osteoarthrosis is present, greatest in the medial femorotibial compartment. In the medial compartment there is diffuse high-grade chondral loss, marginal osteophyte formation, and mild subchondral reactive edema. Moderate chondral thinning is present in the lateral compartment. The patellofemoral compartment shows multifocal chondral fissuring and thinning involving the patellar facets and trochlear groove.

The medial meniscus is degenerative, with complex tearing/fraying of the body and posterior horn and mild extrusion of the body. The lateral meniscus shows degenerative free-edge fraying of the body without a displaced fragment.

The ACL, PCL, MCL, and lateral collateral ligament complex are intact. There is a moderate effusion with mild synovial thickening. A small Baker cyst is present. The extensor mechanism is intact.

IMPRESSION:
1. Tricompartmental osteoarthrosis, worst medially where there is high-grade chondral loss.
2. Degenerative tearing/fraying of the medial meniscus with mild extrusion.
3. Degenerative free-edge fraying of the lateral meniscus.
4. Moderate joint effusion with mild synovitis and small Baker cyst.
